"The Understory" is a fictional small quarterly magazine about city trees. This is the page for one feature article on its website.

Audience and scene: subscribers reading a feature of about 2,000 words in the evening on a laptop or tablet. They chose to be here. The page's job is comfortable sustained reading, keeping one's place, and making one data comparison easy to understand.

Business objective: subscribers renew when they finish the features. The page should make finishing a 2,000-word piece easy and its one data comparison memorable enough to repeat to someone else.

What these readers already hold: the printed quarterly, and field guides to trees.

Content (fictional; use exactly, in this order):
- Section label: Field notes
- Title: The street that kept its elms
- Standfirst: Marlow Row lost none of its 31 elms to the disease that emptied the avenues around it. Nobody planned that.
- Byline line: Words by Petra Lindqvist. Issue 14, Autumn. 9 minute read.
- Paragraph 1: In the spring of 1978 the council's tree officer walked Marlow Row with a clipboard and a tin of yellow paint. He marked nothing. Every other elm-lined street in the ward had at least one tree ringed for felling by then, and most had several.
- Paragraph 2: The usual explanations do not hold. The Row's elms were not a resistant variety, and nobody injected them. What the street had was a gap: a railway cutting to the west and a brickworks to the east, each wide enough to stop the beetles that carry the fungus from tree to tree.
- Subheading: A gap nobody designed
- Paragraph 3: Isolation is not a plan, and the people who lived there did not think of it as one. They thought of the cutting as noise and the brickworks as dust. It took thirty years for anyone to count what was still standing.
- Pull quote: They thought of the cutting as noise and the brickworks as dust.
- Data comparison, titled "Elms standing in 2008, of those recorded in 1970": Marlow Row 31 of 31. Bishop's Avenue 7 of 40. Canal Street 4 of 28. Tanner's Walk 0 of 19.
- Footnote: Counts from the ward tree survey, 1970 and 2008.
