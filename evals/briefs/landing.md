"Spoke & Kettle" is a fictional mobile bicycle workshop in the fictional city of Eastmere. A workshop van parks at an office for a day and services staff bikes while they work. This is its landing page.

Audience and scene: office and facilities managers at companies of 40 to 300 people, reading at a desk between other tasks. They are mildly interested and wary of hassle, cost and commitment. The page's job is to let them decide whether to request a visit day. The one action is "Request a visit day".

Business objective: more visit-day requests from office managers. The commonest reason they do not ask is fear of hassle for themselves, ahead of price.

What these managers already book for the office: window cleaners, fruit deliveries and fire-alarm tests.

Content (fictional; use exactly, and do not add claims):
- What it is: a workshop van that parks at your office for a day and services staff bikes while they work.
- How it works, in order: 1. Pick a day; a visit needs at least 6 bikes booked. 2. Staff book a slot and hand their keys in at the van. 3. Bikes are back in the rack by 5 pm.
- Prices, paid by each rider: Safety check £18. Standard service £55. Full service £95. Parts at cost plus 10%. Nothing is charged to the employer.
- Facts that may be stated: operating since 2019. Two mechanics, Ines Okafor and Tom Reyes. A typical visit services 9 bikes. Labour is guaranteed for 12 months. Covers offices inside the Eastmere ring road.
- Objections to answer: the van needs one parking space and no power supply. There is no contract. A visit can be cancelled up to 48 hours before.
- Do not invent testimonials, client logos, ratings, review counts or any statistic beyond those above. No photography is supplied and none may be faked; use type, layout, diagram or drawn graphics as you see fit.

Representative state for every study: the page as a first-time visitor sees it.
