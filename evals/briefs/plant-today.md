"Sill" is a fictional houseplant care app for phones. This is its Today screen.

Audience and scene: someone with a dozen houseplants, checking the phone in the morning with one hand, for about ten seconds. The screen's job is to show what needs doing today and let them mark it done.

Business objective: the app earns its keep when people open it each morning and their plants survive. It has no interest in keeping them on the screen; a ten-second visit that ends with the right plant watered is the goal.

What this audience already uses every day: a weather app, and a paper wall calendar in the kitchen.

Content (fictional; use exactly, and do not add data):
- Date: Tuesday 3 March. The person has 12 plants.
- Today, 3 tasks:
  1. "Big Greta", a monstera, living room, east window. Water. Last watered 9 days ago.
  2. "Stripes", a calathea, bedroom. Mist. Humidity is low. This task has been skipped twice.
  3. "Beads", a string of pearls, kitchen sill. Water lightly. Last watered 16 days ago.
- Coming up: Thursday, water "Fidel", a fiddle-leaf fig. Saturday, fertilise 4 plants.
- Done yesterday: watered "Ferdinand", a Boston fern.
- Actions on a task: mark done, snooze one day, open the plant.
- No streaks, points, badges, social features or statistics. No photography is supplied and none may be faked; plants may be drawn simply, shown as marks, or carried by type alone, as you see fit.

Representative state for every study: nothing done yet today.
